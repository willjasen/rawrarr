# Rawrarr identity domains

This document is the source of truth for the agreed user, device, group, and managed-app identity boundaries. It separates the current single-owner implementation from planned group membership and app-key lifecycle work. See [device signing design](device-signing-design.md) for the implemented enrollment protocol and [data domains](data-domains.md) for filesystem placement.

## Identity map

| Domain | Owner and creation | Secret/public material | Purpose | Current state |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| User | The account owner creates or imports their post-quantum age identity. Passkeys are separately registered by the person with the coordinator. | The age private identity is kept by the owner and may be remembered locally at `/data/age-identity` with owner-only permissions or in the browser only when explicitly chosen. Its public age recipient may be published. Passkey private keys remain in the user's authenticator; the coordinator stores credential records. | The user identity opens the user-wrapped group identity envelope and encrypts the owner-held root-key backup. In the agreed messaging model, it receives replies addressed to the user. Passkeys authenticate web sessions; they do not encrypt files. | Implemented for the current single-owner deployment. The identity is not recoverable by Rawrarr if lost. |
| Device | Each subscriber creates its age identity and ML-DSA signing key on first start. The owner authorizes the device through an instance-root-signed approval. | Private keys remain node-local under `/data/node-age-identity` and `/data/node-signing-key`. The device age recipient and signing certificate/public key are published through authenticated enrollment. The per-instance ML-DSA root private key is held by the owner as an age-encrypted backup; its public key and signed approvals are stored by the coordinator. | The device age recipient is reserved for messages addressed to that device. The ML-DSA key authenticates enrollment, coordinator requests, and supported signed operational records. These are separate keys and purposes. | Device age and signing key creation, owner approval, and scoped signing are implemented. Node messaging and owner-root rotation/recovery are not. See [device signing design](device-signing-design.md). |
| Group | The owner unlock flow creates or restores a distinct group age identity. Each subscriber can install it from a user-encrypted envelope; automatic approval-time distribution is not implemented. | The group private identity is stored node-locally under `/data/group-age-identity` and wrapped to the user identity in shared ciphertext. It is not a user-to-device or device-to-device message key. | Group-key bootstrap, manual owner unlock, and group-recipient writes are implemented. | Managed app | Rawrarr prepares a distinct identity for each configured Radarr and Sonarr service slot when its settings are saved. Every node that can currently unlock the group identity can decrypt the registry. Activation requires an owner-authorized set of nodes for app-key delivery, not merely possession of the group key. | Intended to encrypt only that app's Rawrarr metadata. It is never used for messaging, shared config, API credentials, or signing. | Preparatory identity generation and persistence are implemented. Metadata remains group-encrypted, and existing ciphertext is not migrated. Authorization-bound app-key delivery, revocation response, activation, ciphertext migration, and recovery are not implemented. These capabilities may reuse #109; unrelated #109 work is not a blanket prerequisite. |

## Key and trust rules

- Age recipients provide confidentiality. An age identity does not authenticate a sender.
- ML-DSA keys provide signatures and authorization evidence. They are not age keys and do not encrypt data.
- Passkeys authenticate a person to the coordinator. They are not age identities, device identities, or signing keys.
- API keys and UI credentials authenticate to Radarr/Sonarr. They remain inside the encrypted app configuration and are not identity keys.
- Never send a private user, device, group, app, or root key to the coordinator. The coordinator may store public keys, signed approvals, ciphertext, and routing metadata required by its protocol; those records are not an independent trust anchor.
- `app-identities.aged` is age ciphertext for the current preparatory release. Older binaries ignore it; it does not change `config.aged` or metadata encryption. Its group-key protection does not provide app-level authorization or revocation.
- Tailscale Serve and coordinator relays may transport node traffic, but the planned messaging service carries age ciphertext only. Network membership does not grant Rawrarr data access.

## Lifecycle and recovery

1. **Bootstrap:** The owner creates or imports a user age identity, sets up the passkey, and retains a recoverable copy. The initial shared folder records the public recipient; ciphertext cannot be recovered by replacing the private identity.
2. **Device approval:** A subscriber creates and persists its own device age and signing keys. The owner verifies the instance-root fingerprint independently and approves the exact pending device request. Approval does not disclose the user's private identity to the node or coordinator.
3. **Group enrollment (planned):** After approval, distribute the current group identity wrapped separately to the new device recipient. The node verifies the current owner-authorized membership state before accepting it. Offline nodes must not regain access using stale configuration.
4. **App identity preparation:** Saving Radarr or Sonarr settings on one owner-selected node creates a UUID-associated app identity in the group-encrypted `app-identities.aged` staging registry. Re-saving existing settings is required to prepare their identities; unlocking or reading old settings does not create shared identity state. Clear operations retain the app identity so later migration can preserve it. Only one node should write this synchronized registry at a time, and the next writer should wait for Syncthing to finish before proceeding. This release leaves `config.aged`, browser decryption, metadata writers, and existing metadata ciphertext unchanged.
5. **App identity activation (planned):** Move the prepared identities into a versioned representation and deliver each only to owner-authorized nodes using a verified trust/authorization mechanism. This may reuse #109, but full completion of #109 is not a blanket prerequisite. Re-encrypt each app's existing metadata only after every participating node and browser path understands app keys and all old writers are fenced. Verify decrypted plaintext equivalence and retain recoverable original ciphertext until the full migration is verified. The current preparatory release does not activate this step.
6. **Revocation (planned):** An owner-authorized revocation stops future app-key delivery and replaces each app identity that the revoked node may have obtained, then migrates the affected metadata. The delivery mechanism must also prevent that node from decrypting future app-key updates; if those updates are protected by a group key the node retains, an additional key-rotation or per-device-wrapping step is required. A revocation cannot erase keys, ciphertext, or plaintext already obtained.
7. **Recovery:** User age identity, instance root backup, node-local device keys, group key, and app keys have separate recovery paths. Rawrarr cannot reconstruct a lost private key from its public recipient or coordinator records. Group/app recovery, owner succession, interrupted rotation, and rejoining after missed changes must fail closed until their protocols are implemented.

Do not interpret the planned lifecycle as available product behavior. Group membership and rotation are tracked by [#109](https://github.com/willjasen/rawrarr/issues/109), per-app identities by [#110](https://github.com/willjasen/rawrarr/issues/110), age-encrypted messaging by [#111](https://github.com/willjasen/rawrarr/issues/111), and managed-app migration by [#112](https://github.com/willjasen/rawrarr/issues/112). Device trust and signed shared-data verification continue in [#40](https://github.com/willjasen/rawrarr/issues/40).
